1| # Elevation Cliff Rendering System
2| 
3| **Date:** 2026-05-27
4| **Status:** Approved
5| **Scope:** Replace flat tile rendering with continuous elevation-driven cliff layers, where terrain height is expressed as stacked cliff walls and elevated surface tiles.
6| 
7| ## Problem
8| 
9| The current hypergraph terrain system selects the correct surface (grass, rock, snow) based on elevation, but renders everything on a single flat plane. A mountain peak and a valley floor sit at the same visual Y position — only the tile color differs. There is no visual topography: no cliffs, no height, no sense of the landscape rising and falling. The world looks like a painted carpet, not terrain.
10| 
11| ## Core Model
12| 
13| Elevation is continuous (0.0–1.0). Every tile's elevation maps to a visual Y-offset in pixels:
14| 
15| ```
16| visual_y_offset = (elevation - SEA_LEVEL) * PIXELS_PER_UNIT
17| ```
18| 
19| Where `SEA_LEVEL = 0.38` and `PIXELS_PER_UNIT = 512` (tunable). This means:
20| - A tile at elevation 0.50 renders 61px above sea level
21| - A tile at elevation 0.80 renders 215px above sea level
22| - A tile at elevation 1.00 renders 317px above sea level
23| - A tile at elevation 0.38 (sea level) renders at Y-offset 0
24| 
25| There are no fixed "levels" or "buckets." Every tile sits at its exact elevation. The number of visual layers in any given area is determined entirely by the terrain — a flat plain has zero cliff faces, a mountain might have cliff walls hundreds of pixels tall.
26| 
27| ## Cliff Walls
28| 
29| When adjacent tiles differ in elevation, the gap between them is filled with a **cliff wall** — a vertical textured face visible from the top-down camera.
30| 
31| ### Which edges get cliff walls
32| 
33| Cliff walls render on the **south and east** edges of elevated tiles. These are the sides visible to a top-down camera with a slight southward perspective (standard for 2D RPGs). North and west edges are occluded by the tile's own surface.
34| 
35| For a tile at (x, y) with elevation E:
36| - **South wall:** if tile at (x, y+1) has elevation E_south < E, draw a cliff wall of height `(E - E_south) * PIXELS_PER_UNIT` pixels on the south edge
37| - **East wall:** if tile at (x+1, y) has elevation E_east < E, draw a cliff wall of height `(E - E_east) * PIXELS_PER_UNIT` pixels on the east edge
38| 
39| ### Cliff wall composition
40| 
41| Each cliff wall is assembled from three tile pieces:
42| 
43| | Piece | Size | Purpose |
44| |-------|------|---------|
45| | `cliff_top` | 32×8px | Overhang where surface meets wall — grass roots, dirt edge, snow lip |
46| | `cliff_mid` | 32×32px | Repeating wall face — stone layers, dirt strata, ice. Tiles vertically. |
47| | `cliff_bot` | 32×8px | Base where wall meets lower ground — shadow, rubble, moss |
48| 
49| For a cliff wall of height H pixels:
50| 1. Draw `cliff_top` (8px)
51| 2. Draw `cliff_mid` repeated `floor((H - 16) / 32)` times (middle fill)
52| 3. Draw remaining fractional `cliff_mid` height (partial tile)
53| 4. Draw `cliff_bot` (8px)
54| 
55| Minimum cliff height to render: 4px (below this, the elevation difference is too small to see).
56| 
57| ### Cliff face variants per surface
58| 
59| Each of the 13 surface types needs its own cliff face set, because the wall should match the terrain it's part of:
60| 
61| | Surface | Cliff face appearance |
62| |---------|----------------------|
63| | lush_grass | Dirt/soil with grass roots hanging over the top edge |
64| | golden_sand | Sandy crumbling cliff face with exposed rock |
65| | forest_floor | Dark earth with tree roots and moss |
66| | grey_rock | Layered stone with mineral veins |
67| | snow | Packed snow over ice with frozen dirt |
68| | frozen_earth | Permafrost layers with ice crystals |
69| | glacial_ice | Blue-white ice face with pressure cracks |
70| | volcanic_rock | Dark basalt with glowing lava veins |
71| | swamp_mud | Wet mud layers with standing water seeping out |
72| | dry_grass | Rocky soil with sparse root systems |
73| | dark_humus | Very dark decomposing layers with fungal growth |
74| | ocean_water | (no cliff face — water fills from below) |
75| | mystic_crystal | Glowing purple crystal cross-section |
76| 
77| That's 12 cliff face sets × 3 pieces = 36 cliff tiles, plus corner and diagonal variants are required for V1:
78| 
79| **Corner variants** (where two cliff walls meet at 90°):
80| - Inner corner (concave) — south wall meets east wall from adjacent tiles
81| - Outer corner (convex) — cliff wraps around a protruding ledge
82| 
83| **Diagonal variants** (where elevation changes diagonally):
84| - SW-NE diagonal cliff face
85| - SE-NW diagonal cliff face
86| 
87| Each surface needs: 3 straight pieces + 2 corner pieces + 2 diagonal pieces = **7 cliff tiles per surface**.
88| 
89| Total: 12 surfaces × 7 pieces = **84 cliff tiles** to generate via PixelLab.
90| 
91| ## Render Order
92| 
93| Tiles render back-to-front using a **painter's algorithm** sorted by (world_y ascending, elevation ascending):
94| 
95| 1. **Water plane** at sea-level Y-offset (if any water visible in the viewport)
96| 2. For each row (north to south):
97|    a. For each tile in the row (sorted by elevation ascending):
98|       - Draw cliff walls (south and east) descending from this tile to lower neighbors
99|       - Draw surface tile at this tile's visual Y-offset
100| 3. Higher tiles naturally occlude lower tiles' cliff walls
101| 
102| This ensures:
103| - Cliffs face the camera (south-facing walls visible)
104| - Higher terrain covers lower terrain's walls
105| - Water sits at the bottom of basins
106| 
107| ## Water
108| 
109| Water is the floor at elevation 0.38 (sea level):
110| 
111| - Any tile with elevation < SEA_LEVEL is submerged — the water surface renders at the sea-level Y plane
112| - The ocean_water surface tile renders ON the water plane for submerged tiles
113| - Coastlines are natural cliff faces: land tiles at elevation > SEA_LEVEL have cliff walls descending to the water plane
114| - Lakes are terrain depressions below sea level
115| - Rivers are narrow channels cut below surrounding terrain elevation
116| 
117| This naturally fixes the overmap/render mismatch — areas the overmap shows as blue (low elevation) will visually be water basins with terrain rising out of them.
118| 
119| ## Camera
120| 
121| - **Follows player** at their visual position (grid position + elevation Y-offset)
122| - **Zoom-out at height**: camera zoom decreases slightly as player elevation increases, giving a wider view from mountaintops. Scale factor: `zoom = base_zoom - (elevation - SEA_LEVEL) * ZOOM_ELEVATION_FACTOR`
123| - **Bokeh/depth-of-field**: terrain below the player's current elevation gets progressively blurred. Already partially implemented — extend to use the new elevation offset system.
124| 
125| ## Player Movement (Deferred)
126| 
127| Not in scope for this spec, but the elevation system enables:
128| 
129| - **Walking**: traverse gentle slopes where adjacent tile elevation difference < MAX_WALK_SLOPE
130| - **Climbing**: BotW/Genshin-style wall climbing on cliff faces (stamina-based)
131| - **Gliding**: jump off cliff edges, trade height for horizontal distance
132| - **Swimming**: enter water at sea level
133| 
134| These will be designed in a separate spec.
135| 
136| ## PIXELS_PER_UNIT Tuning
137| 
138| The single most important constant. Starting value: **512**.
139| 
140| | Scenario | Elevation diff | Visual height |
141| |----------|---------------|---------------|
142| | Gentle slope | 0.02 | 10px (subtle step) |
143| | Small hill | 0.05 | 26px (visible rise) |
144| | Significant hill | 0.15 | 77px (~2.4 tile heights) |
145| | Mountain from plains | 0.40 | 205px (~6.4 tile heights) |
146| | Full sea-to-peak | 0.62 | 317px (~10 tile heights) |
147| 
148| This is data, not code. Tunable at runtime for visual QA.
149| 
150| ## Architecture
151| 
152| ### Modified Components
153| 
154| **`LayeredChunkRenderer`** — Major rewrite. Instead of painting one flat image per chunk, it now:
155| 1. Calculates visual Y-offset per tile from elevation
156| 2. Creates cliff wall sprites between tiles with different elevations
157| 3. Renders surface tiles at their elevated Y positions
158| 4. Sorts everything back-to-front for correct occlusion
159| 
160| **`CleanWorld.gd`** — Camera system updates:
161| - Track player elevation for Y-offset
162| - Zoom-out factor based on elevation
163| - Bokeh intensity based on elevation delta between player and terrain
164| 
165| ### New Components
166| 
167| **`CliffTileLoader`** — Loads cliff face tile assets (cliff_top, cliff_mid, cliff_bot per surface type). Similar to LayeredTilesetLoader but for vertical cliff tiles.
168| 
169| **`ElevationRenderer`** — Core rendering logic extracted from LayeredChunkRenderer. Given a chunk's elevation data, produces the sorted draw list of (surface tiles + cliff walls + water) with correct Y-offsets and z-ordering.
170| 
171| ### New Assets
172| 
173| - `assets/catalog/terrain_v3/cliffs/{surface_id}/cliff_top.png` (32×8)
174| - `assets/catalog/terrain_v3/cliffs/{surface_id}/cliff_mid.png` (32×32)
175| - `assets/catalog/terrain_v3/cliffs/{surface_id}/cliff_bot.png` (32×8)
176| - ~48 total cliff tile images via PixelLab
177| 
178| ### Unchanged Components
179| 
180| - `ElevationGradientTable` — still provides surface_at(biome, elevation)
181| - `HypergraphTileResolver` — still resolves surface + Wang index per tile
182| - `LayeredTilesetLoader` — still loads surface and transition Wang tiles
183| - `ChunkStreamer` — still streams chunks, emits chunk_loaded
184| - `NativeChunkCompiler` (C++) — still produces elevation + biome data
185| 
186| ## Performance Considerations
187| 
188| Each chunk is 64×64 = 4,096 tiles. In the worst case (mountainous terrain), each tile could have south and east cliff walls. That's up to 8,192 cliff wall sprites per chunk plus 4,096 surface sprites = 12,288 draw calls per chunk. With a 7×7 chunk grid visible, that's up to 600K draw calls.
189| 
190| This will require optimization:
191| - **Batch cliff walls** into chunk-level images (like the current surface image approach)
192| - **Skip tiny cliffs** below the 4px minimum threshold
193| - **LOD**: at far zoom, render cliff walls as simple dark lines instead of textured tiles
194| - **Frustum culling**: only render cliff walls visible in the viewport
195| 
196| The GDScript prototype will be slow. This is a strong candidate for C++ migration after the visual design is validated.
