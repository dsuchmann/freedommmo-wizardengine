1016| # Color-Algebra Tile System — Layered Composable Terrain
1017| 
1018| **Date**: 2026-05-25
1019| **Status**: Active
1020| **Depends on**: World Compiler, TileObjectStack, GrainStack (L0)
1021| 
1022| ## Problem
1023| 
1024| Current terrain renders as flat colored squares or non-seamless tile art. No smooth biome transitions, no elevation shading, no ground texture, no visual richness. The world looks like a spreadsheet.
1025| 
1026| ## Solution
1027| 
1028| A composable tile system built from color algebra: 8 base colors × luminance variants × directional gradients × biome transitions × texture patterns × detail objects × interactable items. Each layer is a transparent overlay painted onto the layer below. The layers map to the grain stack — digging removes layers and exposes what's underneath.
1029| 
1030| ## The 8-Color Palette
1031| 
1032| | ID | Color | Hex Range (dark → light) | Biomes |
1033| |----|-------|--------------------------|--------|
1034| | 0 | grass_green | #1a5c1a → #6ed06e | grassland, forest, dense_forest, tropical_forest |
1035| | 1 | water_blue | #0d3b6e → #4da6f0 | ocean, lake, river, swamp |
1036| | 2 | sand_tan | #8a6b3d → #ecc88a | beach, desert, savanna |
1037| | 3 | stone_grey | #3a3a3a → #a8a8a8 | mountains, steppe |
1038| | 4 | snow_white | #c0c8d0 → #f5f7fa | tundra, arctic, taiga |
1039| | 5 | earth_brown | #5a3a1a → #b8906a | paths, roads, dirt, farmland |
1040| | 6 | lava_orange | #8a2a00 → #f0a040 | volcanic |
1041| | 7 | mystic_purple | #3a1a5a → #a060d0 | mushroom_forest, magical zones |
1042| 
1043| Each color has 4 luminance stops: dark (0), mid-dark (1), mid-light (2), light (3).
1044| 
1045| ## 7-Layer Stack (Bottom to Top)
1046| 
1047| ### Layer 0 — Base Color (opaque)
1048| Flat biome tile. One tile per biome, determined by top grain in the GrainStack at that position.
1049| 
1050| - 8 colors × 4 luminance = **32 tiles**
1051| - Default luminance is mid (1 or 2); specific luminance chosen by elevation height band
1052| - PixelLab generation: seamless flat tiles with subtle pixel noise, explicitly "uniform color covering entire tile, no borders, tileable"
1053| 
1054| ### Layer 1 — Luminance Gradient (α 0.3-0.5)
1055| Directional light/dark overlay driven by **elevation slope data**.
1056| 
1057| - Light source assumed top-left
1058| - Slope facing top-left → light gradient overlay
1059| - Slope facing bottom-right → dark gradient overlay
1060| - Steeper slope → higher alpha (more pronounced shading)
1061| - 8 colors × 4 directions (N→S, S→N, W→E, E→W) = **32 tiles**
1062| - Each tile: transparent base with gradient from one luminance stop to another
1063| 
1064| ### Layer 2 — Color Transition (α 0.5-0.8)
1065| Biome boundary blending. Only rendered at tiles where a neighbor has a different biome.
1066| 
1067| - For each tile, check 4 cardinal neighbors
1068| - If neighbor biome differs, overlay a transition tile that blends this biome's color into the neighbor's
1069| - C(8,2) = 28 unique pairs × 4 edge directions = **112 tiles**
1070| - Each tile: one color fading into another across the tile, transparent elsewhere
1071| - Edge matching: transition tile's edge colors must match both biomes' base colors
1072| 
1073| ### Layer 3 — Texture Pattern (α 0.2-0.4)
1074| Seamless tileable noise overlays that add ground texture. Coverage density driven by **vegetation_density** from the compiler.
1075| 
1076| - Each pattern is palette-locked to its compatible base color family
1077| - Placed when vegetation_density > threshold (varies by pattern type)
1078| - High translucency — adds visual variance without obscuring base
1079| 
1080| | Pattern | Compatible Colors | Biomes |
1081| |---------|-------------------|--------|
1082| | grass_blades | grass_green | grassland, forest |
1083| | leaf_litter | grass_green, earth_brown | forest, dense_forest |
1084| | pebble_scatter | stone_grey, earth_brown | mountains, steppe, paths |
1085| | mud_cracks | earth_brown, sand_tan | swamp, farmland |
1086| | sand_ripples | sand_tan | desert, beach |
1087| | moss_patches | grass_green, stone_grey | forest, mountains (wet areas) |
1088| | snow_crystals | snow_white | tundra, arctic |
1089| | water_ripples | water_blue | ocean, lake, river |
1090| 
1091| 8 patterns × 4 variants = **32 tiles**
1092| 
1093| ### Layer 4 — Detail Objects (transparent background, non-interactive)
1094| Individual nature sprites placed by density rules per biome. Decorative only.
1095| 
1096| - Flower clusters, grass tufts, fallen leaves, small stones, roots, puddles, mushroom patches
1097| - Placed where vegetation_density is high enough for the object type
1098| - Palette-coherent with base biome color
1099| - **~50 unique objects**, generated as map_objects in PixelLab (32×32, transparent bg)
1100| 
1101| ### Layer 5 — Interactable Objects (transparent background, pickable)
1102| Items the player can collect. Sparse placement.
1103| 
1104| - Branches, loose stones, herbs, berries, feathers, bones, shells
1105| - Each has: value, use category (crafting material, food, tool component)
1106| - Removed from TileStack when player collects them
1107| - **~30 unique objects**
1108| 
1109| ### Layer 6 — Large Structures (blocking, multi-tile)
1110| Trees, boulders, walls, buildings, furniture. The Lego block system from the tile-object-system spec. Not covered in this spec — already designed separately.
1111| 
1112| ## Grain Stack Binding (Dig Interaction)
1113| 
1114| The visual layer stack mirrors the GrainStack at each tile position:
1115| 
1116| | Grain (top → bottom) | Visual Layer |
1117| |----------------------|--------------|
1118| | grass grain | L0 = grass_green |
1119| | soil grain | L0 = earth_brown (revealed after digging grass) |
1120| | stone grain | L0 = stone_grey (revealed after digging soil) |
1121| | bedrock grain | L0 = stone_grey dark (can't dig further) |
1122| 
1123| **When player digs:**
1124| 1. Pop top grain from GrainStack
1125| 2. Add popped material to player inventory (as TileObject with pickable=true)
1126| 3. Recalculate L0 base color from new top grain
1127| 4. Remove any L3-L5 overlays that depended on the removed grain type
1128| 5. Re-render affected tile layers
1129| 
1130| **When player places material:**
1131| 1. Push grain onto GrainStack
1132| 2. Update L0 base color
1133| 3. Re-render
1134| 
1135| ## Rendering Pipeline (Per Chunk)
1136| 
1137| For each tile (x, y) in the chunk:
1138| 
1139| ```
1140| 1. Read top grain → determine base_color_id and luminance from height band
1141| 2. Paint L0: base flat tile (opaque) onto terrain image
1142| 3. Read slope direction + magnitude → if slope > threshold:
1143|    Paint L1: gradient tile (alpha = slope_magnitude * 0.5) onto gradient image
1144| 4. Check 4 cardinal neighbors for different biome:
1145|    For each differing neighbor, paint L2: transition tile onto transition image
1146| 5. Read vegetation_density → if density > 0.2:
1147|    Select texture pattern by biome, paint L3 onto pattern image (alpha = density * 0.4)
1148| 6. If density > 0.4: place L4 detail object (selected by biome + RNG)
1149| 7. If RNG < 0.05: place L5 interactable object (selected by biome + rarity)
1150| ```
1151| 
1152| Each layer is a separate Image (chunk_size × 32 pixels per side). Total: 6 Image sprites per chunk. With 9 chunks: **54 Sprite2D nodes** (same as current).
1153| 
1154| ## Adjacency Rules
1155| 
1156| Each tile stores 4 edge colors (N, S, E, W) derived from its content:
1157| - Flat tiles: all 4 edges = same luminance stop
1158| - Gradient tiles: edges match the luminance at that edge of the gradient
1159| - Transition tiles: one pair of edges = color A, opposite pair = color B
1160| 
1161| The renderer enforces: **adjacent tiles' touching edges must have the same color value.** This guarantees no visible seams.
1162|

1224| # Layer Architecture Specification
1225| 
1226| > Established 2026-05-25. This is the authoritative reference for the rendering layer system.
1227| > All implementation must conform to this spec. Do not re-derive — reference this document.
1228| 
1229| ## Core Principle: Animation Cel Model
1230| 
1231| The world is composed like traditional animation cels — transparent sheets stacked on top of each other. Each layer is a separate sheet. Some sheets are fully painted (opaque), most have sparse content with transparency showing layers beneath.
1232| 
1233| The player sees one composite image. We know it's 8 separate layers.
1234| 
1235| ## Layer Stack
1236| 
1237| | Z | Layer | Blend Mode | Coverage | Purpose |
1238| |---|-------|-----------|----------|---------|
1239| | -2 | **Terrain** | Opaque fill | 100% of tiles | Base color — solid green/brown/blue/gray. Every pixel covered. |
1240| | -1 | **Shading** | Multiply | 100% of tiles | Elevation shadows, hillshade, ambient occlusion. Darkens/lightens terrain beneath. Never fully opaque. |
1241| | 0 | **Water** | Opaque where present | Sparse (~15-25%) | Rivers, lakes, ocean. Replaces terrain visually. Transparent elsewhere. Animated (ripple frames). |
1242| | 1 | **Ground Detail** | Transparent overlay | Dense (~60-80%) | Grass blades, pebbles, fallen leaves, sand ripples, moss patches. You see terrain between them. |
1243| | 2 | **Paths/Roads** | Opaque where present | Sparse (~5-10%) | Dirt trails, cobblestone roads, bridges. Covers terrain + ground detail where path exists. |
1244| | 3 | **Buildings** | Opaque where present | Sparse (~3-8%) | Floors, walls, doors, windows, interior furniture. Covers everything beneath. |
1245| | 4-5 | **Objects** | Transparent sprites | Moderate (~20-40%) | Trees, bushes, rocks, signs, wells, carts, flowers, branches. Sprites have transparent backgrounds. Y-sorted (front overlaps back). |
1246| | 6 | **Roofs** | Conditional alpha | Sparse (building footprints only) | Building canopies. Opaque normally, alpha-fades when player enters building. |
1247| | 7 | **Lighting** | Multiply/Additive | 100% of tiles | Day/night tint, torchlight radii, point light glow. Tints everything beneath. Never fully opaque. |
1248| 
1249| ## Blend Mode Definitions
1250| 
1251| - **Opaque fill**: Every pixel in this layer is drawn. Nothing beneath is visible.
1252| - **Opaque where present**: Where a tile exists on this layer, it fully covers what's beneath. Where no tile exists, the layer is invisible (pass-through).
1253| - **Transparent overlay**: Sprites/tiles are placed on this layer but have transparent backgrounds. You see through to all layers beneath between the drawn pixels.
1254| - **Multiply**: Darkens the composite beneath. White = no effect, black = full shadow. Used for lighting/shading.
1255| - **Additive**: Brightens the composite beneath. Black = no effect. Used for light sources.
1256| - **Conditional alpha**: Normally opaque, but fades to transparent based on game state (e.g., player enters building).
1257| 
1258| ## Layer -2: Terrain (Detail)
1259| 
1260| ### What it IS
1261| 
1262| A smooth continuous field of three values per tile, rendered as opaque colored tiles:
1263| 
1264| | Per-tile value | Range | Source |
1265| |---|---|---|
1266| | Elevation | 0.0 – 1.0 | Perlin noise |
1267| | Temperature | 0.0 – 1.0 | Perlin noise + latitude gradient |
1268| | Precipitation | 0.0 – 1.0 | Perlin noise + elevation influence |
1269| 
1270| Derived values (computed from the above):
1271| - **Slope**: magnitude of elevation gradient between neighbors
1272| - **Fertility**: derived from precipitation + temperature + slope
1273| - **Ocean distance**: BFS from ocean cells
1274| - **Water distance**: BFS from river/lake cells
1275| 
1276| ### Biome Labels
1277| 
1278| The continuous values get classified into 15 biome labels for convenience. These are NOT hard visual boundaries — they're indexes that higher layers use to select content.
1279| 
1280| | Biome | Elevation | Temperature | Precipitation | Visual Base |
1281| |---|---|---|---|---|
1282| | Deep Ocean | < 0.15 | — | — | Dark blue |
1283| | Ocean | 0.15–0.25 | — | — | Blue |
1284| | Shallow Water | 0.25–0.32 | — | — | Light blue |
1285| | Beach | 0.32–0.37 | — | — | Tan/yellow |
1286| | Grassland | mid | 0.3–0.6 | 0.3–0.55 | Green |
1287| | Forest | mid | > 0.4 | > 0.55 | Dark green |
1288| | Dense Forest | mid | 0.3–0.6 | > 0.75 | Very dark green |
1289| | Desert | mid-low | > 0.6 | < 0.3 | Sandy yellow |
1290| | Savanna | mid | > 0.6 | 0.2–0.5 | Yellow-green |
1291| | Steppe | mid | 0.3–0.6 | < 0.3 | Brown-green |
1292| | Hills | 0.65–0.82 | — | — | Olive/rocky |
1293| | Mountains | > 0.82 | > 0.3 | — | Gray |
1294| | Tundra | high or cold | < 0.25–0.3 | — | White-blue |
1295| | Taiga | — | 0.15–0.3 | > 0.4 | Dark blue-green |

1303| ### Biome Labels Are Convenience Indexes, Not Boundaries
1304| 
1305| The underlying data is continuous. Two adjacent tiles may have nearly identical temperature/moisture but fall on different sides of a classification threshold. Visually, they should look nearly identical — the transition is smooth via Wang tilesets, not a hard edge.
1306| 
1307| Higher layers query the biome label when they need a quick "what kind of place is this?" answer (e.g., "should I place oak trees or cacti here?"). They can also query the raw floats for finer-grained decisions.
1308| 
1309| ### Elevation as Visual Information
1310| 
1311| Elevation is communicated visually through multiple mechanisms:
1312| - **This layer (Layer -2)**: Tile selection shifts — higher elevation gets rockier, exposed stone at peaks
1313| - **Layer -1 (Shading)**: Hillshade — darker on north-facing slopes, lighter on south-facing
1314| - **Layer 1 (Ground Detail)**: Fewer grass blades on steep slopes, more exposed rock
1315| - **Layer 4-5 (Objects)**: Tree density decreases with elevation, different species at altitude
1316| 
1317| This is TERRAIN elevation (continuous, natural landscape). STRUCTURAL elevation (stairs, second floors, raised platforms) is a separate system on Layer 3.
1318| 
1319| ### What This Layer Does NOT Do
1320| 
1321| - Does not know about buildings, paths, or any man-made content
1322| - Does not carve holes for settlements
1323| - Does not render visual detail (that's Layer 1)
1324| - Does not determine walkability by itself (walkability comes from combining multiple layers)
1325| 
1326| ## How Layers Interact
1327| 
1328| ### Information flows DOWN → UP
1329| 
1330| Lower layers don't know about higher layers. Higher layers READ from lower layers.
1331| 
1332| ```
1333| Layer -2 (Terrain: elevation, temp, precip)
1334|   → Layer -1 reads elevation for shading
1335|   → Layer 0 reads elevation for water placement (below sea level = water)
1336|   → Layer 1 reads biome + fertility for ground detail selection
1337|   → Layer 2 reads terrain for road routing (prefer flat, avoid water)
1338|   → Layer 3 reads terrain for building placement (flat areas, near water, avoid flood zones)
1339|   → Layer 4-5 reads biome for object selection (oaks in forest, cacti in desert)
1340|   → Layer 7 reads elevation for fog density (valleys = foggy)
1341| ```
1342| 
1343| ### Visual compositing flows UP → DOWN (painter's algorithm)
1344| 
1345| Render from bottom to top. Each layer paints over what's beneath based on its blend mode.
1346| 
1347| ### Occlusion rules
1348| 
1349| - Opaque layers HIDE everything beneath them where they have content
1350| - Transparent layers ADD to what's beneath without hiding it
1351| - Building floors (Layer 3) hide terrain (Layer -2) and ground detail (Layer 1)
1352| - But objects (Layer 4-5) near buildings are removed by the SETTLEMENT SYSTEM, not by visual occlusion — you don't place flowers inside a tavern because the system knows not to, not because the floor hides them
1353| 
1354| ## The Grassland Example (All Layers)
1355| 
1356| What the player sees when looking at a grassy hillside with a nearby building:
1357| 
1358| 1. **Layer -2**: Solid green fill (base terrain — every pixel)
1359| 2. **Layer -1**: Subtle darkening on the north slope, slight brightening on south (multiply)
1360| 3. **Layer 0**: Nothing here (no water)
1361| 4. **Layer 1**: Dense grass blade sprites scattered across the green. A few pebbles near the hill crest. (transparent overlay — green shows between blades)
1362| 5. **Layer 2**: A dirt path cutting through (opaque — covers green + grass where it exists)
1363| 6. **Layer 3**: Tavern floor/walls (opaque — covers everything beneath within footprint)
1364| 7. **Layer 4-5**: Wildflowers in the grass. A fallen branch. Three oak trees. A sign by the path. (transparent sprites — y-sorted, you see through to grass between them)
1365| 8. **Layer 6**: Red thatch roof over the tavern (opaque, would alpha-fade if player enters)
1366| 9. **Layer 7**: Warm afternoon tint, torch glow from tavern windows (multiply/additive)
1367| 
1368| Each layer animates independently:
1369| - Grass blades sway (Layer 1)
1370| - Flowers bob slightly (Layer 4)
1371| - Torch light flickers (Layer 7)
1372| - Water ripples if present (Layer 0)
1373| 
1374| ## Design Philosophy
1375| 
1376| ### Layer -2 is primarily INFORMATION, secondarily visual
1377| 
1378| Its main job is providing the continuous terrain data that all other layers query. The visual rendering (solid colored tiles) is a baseline that's mostly hidden by the richer layers above it. But it must cover every pixel — it's the "canvas" that guarantees no gaps.
1379| 
1380| ### Higher layers ENRICH, lower layers INFORM
1381| 
1382| Layer -2 tells Layer 1 "this is grassland with 0.8 fertility" → Layer 1 places dense grass blades.
1383| Layer -2 tells Layer 4 "this is forest with 0.6 moisture" → Layer 4 places oak trees with ferns at their base.
1384| 
1385| ### Additive vs Replacement is per-layer, not per-tile
1386| 
1387| A layer's blend mode is fixed. Ground Detail (Layer 1) is ALWAYS transparent overlay. Buildings (Layer 3) are ALWAYS opaque where present. You don't decide per-tile whether to add or replace — the layer's role determines it.
1388| 
1389| ### The settlement system clears higher layers, not the terrain
1390| 
1391| When a building is placed, the settlement system removes grass blades (Layer 1) and objects (Layer 4-5) from within the footprint. It does NOT modify Layer -2. The terrain data persists beneath the building for:
1392| - Biome context ("the tavern is in a grassland")
1393| - Destruction recovery (if building burns, terrain is revealed)
1394| - Gameplay systems that query terrain regardless of structures
