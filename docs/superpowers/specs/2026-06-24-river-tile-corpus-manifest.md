## Aesthetic
A damp working-river hamlet of watermills, jetty cottages and bankside houses, every wall reading first as one solid load-bearing surface keyed to constant splash and a humid bank. The architectural identity is WET RIVERSIDE VERNACULAR — water-worn round cobbles bedded in cream daub, pitch-tarred vertical millboards, dark-oak frames packed with mid grey-cream plaster, and squared wet gorge-ashlar — every wall damp at the foot with a dark waterline tide-mark and creeping moss. Value comes from dark mortar joints, tar-black members, deep chinking shadow and the wet mossy footing, NEVER from a bright body; the lightest face is a MID grey-cream troweled plaster, never near-white. Palette: water-worn grey-tan cobble, cream lime daub, pitch-black tarred oak with honey battens, mid grey-cream plaster over dark chestnut-oak, wet grey-green ashlar with dark mortar, all flecked with green moss, damp lichen and algae tide-stains.
